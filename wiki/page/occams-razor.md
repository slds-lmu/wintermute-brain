## Definition

Occam's razor, also called the principle or law of parsimony, is the
problem-solving principle that recommends searching for explanations
constructed with the smallest possible set of elements. It is frequently
cited as "Entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity" and popularly
paraphrased as "of two competing theories, the simpler explanation of an
entity is to be preferred"
[Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## Scope

The razor applies when competing hypotheses make the same prediction and
have equal explanatory power: then one should prefer the hypothesis that
requires the fewest assumptions. It is not meant to choose between
hypotheses that make different predictions, and it is not a recommendation
of the simplest theory come what may. In science it serves as a heuristic
for developing theoretical models, not as an arbiter between published
models; the simplest explanation consistent with current data may be ruled
out as new data become available
[Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## Origin

The principle is attributed to William of Ockham (c. 1287–1347), an English
Franciscan friar and philosopher, but he did not invent it and never used
the words usually quoted. Similar statements go back to Aristotle and were
commonplace in 13th-century scholastic writing; the popular wording was
formulated by John Punch in 1639
[Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## Why prefer simpler explanations

- **Probability:** all assumptions introduce possibilities for error; an
  assumption that does not improve the accuracy of a theory only increases
  the probability that the theory is wrong
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Bayesian inference:** Jefferys and Berger (1991) state, "A hypothesis
  with fewer adjustable parameters will automatically have an enhanced
  posterior probability, due to the fact that the predictions it makes are
  sharp." According to MacKay, this needs no prior bias in favor of simpler
  models
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Testability:** for every accepted explanation there are many more
  complex alternatives, and a failing explanation can always be saved with
  ad hoc hypotheses. Popper prefers simpler theories because they apply to
  more cases and are more easily falsifiable
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Track record:** a study of the razor's predictive validity found 32
  published papers with 97 comparisons of forecasts from simple and complex
  methods. None showed that complexity improved accuracy; in the 25 papers
  with quantitative comparisons, complexity increased forecast errors by an
  average of 27 percent
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## In statistics and machine learning

- **Overfitting:** excessively complex models are affected by statistical
  noise, whereas simpler models may capture the underlying structure better
  and predict better. The bias–variance tradeoff incorporates the razor in
  its balance between overfitting and underfitting
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Bayesian model comparison:** the original razor only compares equally
  good models. Bayes factors give a more general form that also compares
  models that do not fit the observations equally well. The exact Occam
  factor is generally intractable; approximations include the Akaike
  information criterion, the Bayesian information criterion, variational
  Bayesian methods and Laplace's method
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Description length:** Solomonoff's theory of universal inductive
  inference, founded around 1960, is a mathematical formalization of the
  razor. Related formal versions use Kolmogorov complexity, minimum
  description length and minimum message length (MML): "the sum of the log
  universal probability of the model plus the log of the probability of the
  data given the model should be minimized."
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Deep learning:** deep neural networks are reported to prefer simpler
  mathematical functions while learning, a simplicity bias that helps them
  overcome overfitting
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## Criticism and limits

- **No free lunch:** the no free lunch theorems for inductive inference
  prove that the razor must rely on ultimately arbitrary assumptions about
  the prior probability distribution: an anti-Bayes procedure that predicts
  the opposite of an Occam-based Bayesian procedure wins under just as many
  priors as it loses
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Simplicity is not uniquely defined:** statistical versions need a
  specific definition of simplicity, and that definition can vary; in the
  description-length approach one could pick a Turing machine under which
  one's own theory scores as simple. This has led to an objective and a
  subjective camp. A theory can also become simpler in its ontology while
  its structure becomes more complex, or vice versa
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Wrong calls:** appeals to simplicity were used to argue against
  meteorites, ball lightning, continental drift and reverse transcriptase,
  and atomic theory was first considered the more complex explanation.
  Science has repeatedly shown that future data often support more complex
  theories than existing data do
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Biology:** Francis Crick warns, "While Ockham's razor is a useful tool
  in the physical sciences, it can be a very dangerous implement in biology.
  It is thus very rash to use simplicity and elegance as a guide in
  biological research."
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Too much simplification:** an often-quoted constraint, which cannot be
  verified as Einstein's own, reads "Everything should be kept as simple as
  possible, but not simpler." Anti-razors make the same point, for example
  Walter Chatton's "If three things are not enough to verify an affirmative
  proposition about things, a fourth must be added and so on."
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **No general justification:** Elliott Sober holds that parsimony has
  meaning only when embedded in a specific context of inquiry, and that
  there may be no non-circular justification for it
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
