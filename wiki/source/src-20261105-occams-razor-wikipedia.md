## Summary

The English Wikipedia article on Occam's razor, the principle of parsimony:
when competing hypotheses make the same prediction and explain equally well,
prefer the one that requires the fewest assumptions. The article covers the
history of the principle, its justifications, its formal versions in
probability theory and statistics, its uses across fields, and its critics
[Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).

## Key points

- **Statement:** the razor is frequently cited as "Entities must not be
  multiplied beyond necessity", although Occam never used those words. It
  applies to hypotheses with equal explanatory power and is not meant to
  choose between hypotheses that make different predictions. In science it
  is an abductive heuristic for developing models, not a rigorous arbiter
  between candidate models
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **History:** the principle is attributed to William of Ockham (c.
  1287–1347) but is older; the article traces it to Aristotle, Ptolemy,
  Robert Grosseteste, Duns Scotus and Thomas Aquinas. The popular wording
  was formulated by John Punch in 1639, and Libert Froidmont claimed in 1649
  to have coined the phrase "novacula occami"
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Justifications:** aesthetic and theological ones before the 20th
  century; since then mainly epistemological ones based on induction, logic,
  pragmatism and probability theory. Popper justifies the preference for
  simple theories by falsifiability: they are better testable
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Probabilistic argument:** every assumption adds a possibility for error.
  Jefferys and Berger (1991) state, "A hypothesis with fewer adjustable
  parameters will automatically have an enhanced posterior probability, due
  to the fact that the predictions it makes are sharp." MacKay stresses that
  a prior bias in favor of simpler models is not required
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Statistics and machine learning:** the article links the razor to
  overfitting and the bias–variance tradeoff, Bayesian model comparison with
  Bayes factors, approximations of the Occam factor (Akaike information
  criterion, Bayesian information criterion, variational Bayesian methods,
  Laplace's method), Kolmogorov complexity, minimum description length,
  minimum message length and Solomonoff's theory of inductive inference. It
  also reports a simplicity bias of deep neural networks
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Empirical test:** a study of forecasting methods found 32 published
  papers with 97 comparisons of simple and complex methods; in the 25 papers
  with quantitative comparisons, complexity increased forecast errors by an
  average of 27 percent
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Criticism and limits:** the no free lunch theorems show that the razor
  rests on ultimately arbitrary assumptions about the prior; the definition
  of simplicity can vary (objective vs. subjective razor); Francis Crick
  calls the razor "a very dangerous implement in biology"; simplicity
  arguments were used against meteorites, continental drift and atomic
  theory; and several philosophers formulated anti-razors (Chatton, Leibniz,
  Kant, Menger)
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
- **Other uses** covered by the article and not carried into the wiki:
  biology (units of selection, cladistic parsimony), philosophy of religion,
  philosophy of mind, penal ethics, and the rule of least power in software
  development
  [Occam's razor (Wikipedia)](/wiki/source/src-20261105-occams-razor-wikipedia.md).
